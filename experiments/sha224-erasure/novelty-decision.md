# Final-round erasure: concrete adoption decision

2026-10-12. The implemented final-round rule is **not adopted** as the requested
new algorithm. Under the user's criteria it is Level 3: a direct algebraic
rewrite exploiting an unused final output. This does not assert that a paper
has published the identical WAT, nor that every information-erasing transform
is known. Correctness and the previous execution evidence are unaffected.

## 1. Operation-by-operation reduction of the implemented rule

Let C contain the other terms of T1. The relevant original computation is

```
u = e & f
v = (~e) & g
T1 = h + (u | v) + C
h_next = g
```

The transformed computation is generated by the following complete recipe:

1. Use the disjoint supports of u and v to rewrite u|v as u+v.
2. Reassociate modular addition: h+(u+v) = (h+v)+u.
3. Assign h_bar=h+v. The contribution of the old g to T1 is now in h_bar.
4. In this final round only, h_next is discarded by the final seven-word
   observation. There are no other uses of the old g after forming h_bar.
5. Set g_bar=0 and express the unchanged sum as
   h_bar+Ch(e,f,g_bar)+C. Then h_next=g_bar may be discarded.

Steps 1, 2 and 5 are elementary identities; steps 3 and 4 are temporary
introduction and a use/observability analysis. This recipe gives the exact
implemented assignments, including their order. No additional rule remains
unexplained. The rejection therefore does not depend on the abstract label
"observability relation", an easy inverse, an optimizer, or an attack result.

The rule can remain a useful correct-hash transformation example, but further
tests, earlier placement, random replacement values or a new name would not
repair this concrete novelty deficiency.

## 2. General transported projection: what the formula does and does not show

Fix a final padded block, its schedule W and incoming chaining value B. Let
T_i be the reversible working-state transition, and R_i=T_63 ... T_i the
remaining rounds (R_64=I). Let pi select the first seven words and let
j(q)=(q,0), so P=j pi. Define

```
q_i(S) = pi R_i(S)
r_i(q) = R_i^-1 j(q)
A_i(S) = r_i(q_i(S)).
```

Then q_i r_i=I, A_i^2=A_i, and

```
q_(i+1)(T_i(S)) = q_i(S)
T_i(r_i(q)) = r_(i+1)(q).
```

The seven-word quotient coordinate q is therefore **constant across rounds**.
It is precisely the already-computed remaining-round observation, before
adding the first seven fixed words of B. Its existence alone is not a new
method for evaluating those rounds. Directly evaluating q_i and r_i performs
the suffix computation and an inverse suffix computation.

This is an algebraic characterization, not a literature-based proof that
every possible efficient implementation of q_i or r_i has been published.
In particular, a genuinely independent way to generate or compute an earlier
boundary representation would require its own assessment. The current prototype
only simplifies A_63 using the recipe in section 1; it supplies no such method.

## 3. Different resets and noncommuting insertions do not by themselves help

Consider even a general output-preserving terminal map

```
F(q,t) = (q, f(q,t)),
```

which need not be injective, idempotent or constant in t. Transport it to a
boundary as E_i=R_i^-1 F R_i. For i<j write U=T_(j-1)...T_i, so R_i=R_j U.
Inserting F_i at boundary i and F_j at boundary j gives

```
R_j E_j U E_i = F_j F_i R_i.
```

Thus multiple transported modifications, even noncommuting ones, are exactly
the composition of their terminal modifications. With terminal maps preserving
q, the output is preserved. This extends the earlier idempotent-collapse
observation: removing idempotence does not itself introduce an independent
meaning-preservation mechanism. It also does not prove all implementations
equivalent as instruction sequences or rule out a new construction elsewhere.
