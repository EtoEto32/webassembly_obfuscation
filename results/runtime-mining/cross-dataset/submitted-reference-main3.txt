Analysis of Public WebAssembly Datasets
Author Name 1
Abstract: Static indicators such as branch tables, indirect calls, and redundant instructions do not by
themselves establish obfuscation in WebAssembly. We analyze public cryptojacking-related datasets and
general-purpose comparison corpora to distinguish obfuscating transformations from ordinary computation
and research instrumentation. Following a 35-binary pilot, we examine 20 paired-control ﬁles from eight
diﬀerent programs and eight exploratory corpus ﬁles. Removing instrumentation increments restores all 147
original functions in A-Star and all 756 in CreaturePack at the decoded-instruction level. State-dispatch
payloads are matched to original computations in base64-cli and seqtk. Flattening remains unconﬁrmed at
the inspected sites in the eight exploratory ﬁles, where input- or type-dependent processing remains an al-
ternative explanation. A 2,049-entry branch table implements ordinary numeric processing, whereas veriﬁed
ﬂattening examples update a state variable and return to a dispatcher. Functions matching SEISMIC in-
strumentation occur in 121 malware-labeled and three benign-labeled WasmMal modules. Paired examples
from W ASMixer conﬁrm ﬂattening, call indirection, renaming, and memory-access indirection; a Cryptic
Bytes example also conﬁrms ﬂattening. Related public binaries contain locally redundant arithmetic, with-
out establishing its intent or transformation history. Our ﬁndings separate observed indicators from veriﬁed
mechanisms and provide concrete evidence for interpreting obfuscation in public datasets.
